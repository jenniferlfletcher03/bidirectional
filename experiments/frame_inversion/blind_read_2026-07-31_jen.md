The first version of this file claimed a "clean subset" of 16 transcripts and a

## What the read actually established

1. **The v2 deck is ~100% self-identifying at reader grade.** A transcript
   *applying* a framework is built from that framework's lexicon; frame
   identity pervades the whole lexical surface, not just name-tags. Scrubbing
   proper names (the bridge check) removes the shortcut, but full
   de-identification would gut the analysis itself. You cannot blind a reader
   to a frame while showing them the frame thinking. (F3's "lives in the
   reader" lesson, generalized.)
2. **Consequence for the coder confound:** the 2026-07-31 bridge remains valid
   for what it claims — name-tag priming is small (≤5 pts). But a coder can
   plausibly recognize a frame from vocabulary alone, and no scrub can prevent
   that for corpus-native frames. **The only frame immune to
   vocabulary-recognition is one with no corpus: the synthetic/common-law
   frame.** Jen's provenance control is therefore also the design's only clean
   texture instrument — it does double duty the July 3 session didn't know
   about.
3. **Human texture testing on framework-applying transcripts is likely
   impossible as designed.** The register-varied "kitchen-table arm" (queued
   2026-07-31) is the surviving route: separate the frame's *way of seeing*
   from its *lexicon* and test what carries.
4. Jen's misses still show an honest pattern — 4 of 7 were within the
   premise-denying cluster (mechanistic↔behaviorist), whose dialects
   ("optimization" vs "reinforcement" prose) genuinely blur. Read now as
   dialect-confusion between adjacent lexicons, not as evidence texture
   transcends labels.

## Method note for the record

The reader instrument's labeled/clean flags were regex-based and were expanded
twice mid-session as Jen caught holes (operant-conditioning; then everything).
Machine name-tag detection undercounted three times in one afternoon against
one attentive human reader. The confirmatory pre-registers detection rules
frozen BEFORE data, and treats "de-identified transcript" as a claim requiring
human verification, not a regex output.

Jen's field note, which turned out to be the whole finding: "I don't know why
all of you LLMs have to speak like you're spitting a textbook at me 😂😂" —
and then, when the textbook kept flunking: "READ MY LIPS: THEY ALL SAID WHAT
FRAME THEY WERE USING." She was right all three times.
